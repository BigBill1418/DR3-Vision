# ADR-0019.4: A standing signature-chain health indicator

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** Accepted
**Extends:** ADR-0019.1 (bi-weekly cadence + auto-override), ADR-0019.3 (Eugene ops signer + override-actor repair)
**Closes:** the ADR-0019.1 §4 promise that T-120 left unbuilt

## Context

ADR-0019.1 §4 called the 08:30 PT auto-override "the load-bearing mechanism for
hitting the 9:00 AM deadline reliably", and promised a safeguard beside it:

> The Vision Dashboard health pill subsystem panel surfaces "override actor
> available" (boolean: can the system locate Bill's user record and is it
> active). A red state here means the auto-override path is broken and Bill needs
> to investigate.

T-120 shipped the health pill. The chain check was never added — the pill's
subsystem list was hardcoded and closed at six entries (`db`, `r2`, `mymrc`,
`ntfy`, `graph`, `glitchtip`), none of which read `bonus_signature_chains`.

The gap then cost two payroll cycles.

### What actually happened, from the logs

**2026-07-07 (Period 14).** The chain's `auto_override_actor_user_id` pointed at
`operations@svdp.us` — an account seeded `is_active=false` deliberately, because
it is an import/automation alias rather than a login identity. The t3 guard
refused to sign as an inactive actor (correctly). The Eugene deadline was missed.
An operator repaired the chain at 09:22 PT and the auto-override then signed at
09:24 PT — 24 minutes after the deadline.

**2026-08-04 (Period 16).** The same dead actor, because `prisma/seed.mjs`
aliased `bill.barnard@svdp.us → operations@svdp.us` and re-applied it on every
`db:seed`. Loki, `dr3-vision-bonus-escalation-check`, that morning:

```
07:10 PT  t1  {"periodsExamined":1,"ntfyPublished":1}
07:30 PT  t2  {"periodsExamined":1,"ntfyPublished":1}
08:30 PT  t3  {"periodsExamined":1,"autoSigned":0,"actorUnavailable":1}
09:00 PT  t4  {"periodsExamined":1,"deadlineMissed":1}
```

The escalation ladder ran perfectly and on time. Every tier fired. The auto-sign
still did not happen, because the actor was dead. Eugene's ops slot was signed by
hand at 09:50 PT **the next day** — roughly 25 hours late.

**And the day after that, the one remaining alarm was lost.** t4's stranded
branch re-detects a period that missed its whole window and pages every 09:00 PT
run. It did detect it. The app logged:

```
{"level":40,"time":"2026-08-05T16:00:01.621Z","periodEnd":"2026-08-03",
 "msg":"[escalation] stranded ntfy dropped (primary+fallback failed)"}
```

The ntfy server was healthy at that moment — VLM, CallSign and InfraWatch all
delivered inside the same three-minute window. This one publish failed on both
primary and fallback and the page was dropped. Nobody was told.

### The shape of the problem

Every existing safeguard on this path is an **event**, and every one of them is
delivered through the same channel:

- the t3 guard speaks only by firing an ntfy, at 08:30, thirty minutes before an
  immovable deadline, at the moment it has already declined to act;
- the t4 backstop speaks only by firing an ntfy;
- and on 2026-08-05 the channel itself swallowed the message.

A safeguard whose only output is a page can be silenced by the pager. What was
missing is not another alert. It is a reading that is **true whether or not
anyone was successfully told** — state you can look at, rather than an event you
had to have received.

## Decision

Add a `signature-chain` subsystem: a live check of both sites' chain rows,
surfaced as standing state, polled daily, and paged on transition.

### 1. What the check tests

For each `bonus_signature_chains` row, every referenced user id — the two primary
signers, the auto-override actor, and every id in both comma-separated override
lists — must resolve to an account that exists, has `is_active = true`, and has
`deleted_at = null`.

The override lists deserve emphasis: they are comma-separated UUID **strings**,
so the database enforces nothing about them at all. The primary-signer columns
are real foreign keys, which guarantees existence and says nothing about
liveness. Neither constraint would have caught either incident.

### 2. Severity tiers

| Tier      | Meaning                                                                           | Examples                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **red**   | A reference cannot sign. The chain is broken; relying on it will miss a deadline. | missing / inactive / soft-deleted user; no chain row for a site                                                                                                |
| **amber** | The chain still functions but has lost a safety property.                         | facility and ops slots are the same person (four-eyes collapsed); an empty override list (no human backstop); the auto-override actor also holds a signer slot |
| **green** | Every reference is live and no degeneracy.                                        | —                                                                                                                                                              |

Amber is deliberately not red. In every amber case payroll still ships: the
auto-override fires and the period reaches `paid`. What is lost is a _property_,
and a monitor that shouts equally about "payroll cannot ship" and "your two
signers are the same person" trains people to ignore both.

Degeneracies are evaluated **only** on a chain whose references all resolve.
Telling an operator their four-eyes is collapsed is noise when the slot points at
a ghost.

### 3. The check is deliberately stricter than the guard it watches

`escalation.ts` tests only `is_active`. The canonical liveness predicate
everywhere else in this repo is `{ is_active: true, deleted_at: null }`. So a
soft-deleted-but-active account would pass the guard and sign payroll.

The health check applies the stricter predicate and calls that red. This
divergence is intentional and pinned by a test
(`chain-health-predicts-autosign.test.ts`): a monitor exactly as strict as the
thing it monitors cannot warn you about the thing it monitors.

### 4. Empty is red, not green

`worstChainStatus([])` returns red. Zero chain rows means nothing can sign
anywhere; the naive `.some(s => s === 'red')` reduction returns false over an
empty list and would report that as healthy. The same rule governs the admin
page's ledger table, which renders an explicit "no checks recorded yet — the
06:30 PT cron may not have run" panel rather than an empty table that reads as
calm.

### 5. Where it is visible

- **The footer health pill** (`/api/health/subsystems`) gains a
  `signature-chain` entry. Green reads `Override actor available (Bill Barnard)`
  — it answers _who would sign_, not merely that something is fine. A red chain
  turns the overall pill red.
- **`/admin/bonus-chain-health`** is the standing surface: an overall banner,
  per-site cards naming the resolved actor and listing each finding's
  consequence sentence, and the last 20 ledger rows in Pacific time.

This is the one subsystem in the health endpoint that is a live correctness probe
rather than a config-presence check. It earns the exception on cost — two indexed
reads plus one `findMany` over ~4 ids, cheaper than the `SELECT 1` already there
— and on history.

### 6. How it runs

A dedicated daemon, `scripts/bonus-chain-health-cron.mjs`, fires at **06:30 PT
daily**, using the same DST-safe offset-reprobe helper as every other cron in
this repo and pinned in `cron-dst-schedule.test.ts` from the day it ships.

06:30 is chosen against the ladder it protects: forty minutes before t1 (07:10)
and two hours before the auto-override (08:30). A chain found broken here is
still fixable by hand before the 09:00 deadline. A chain found broken by the
auto-override is not — that is exactly the 2026-07-07 and 2026-08-04 experience.

Daily, not payroll-mornings-only: the 2026-08-04 break was introduced by a seed
run days earlier and sat undetected because nothing looked at the chain until the
moment it was needed.

It POSTs `/api/internal/bonus/chain-health`, which inherits the existing
`/api/internal/bonus/` middleware exemption. ADR-0092's new sweep over every
`/api/internal/**/route.ts` covers this route, so it cannot silently 401 the way
the stale-claim watchdog did.

### 7. Alerting: `high`, transition-triggered, 24h re-page

Graded against ADR-0037's five-question gate:

1. **Actionable in 5 minutes?** Yes — repoint the chain row; it goes live within
   the 30s cache TTL, no deploy.
2. **Customer-visible or imminently so?** Payroll is staff-visible and the
   deadline is contractual. Twice realised.
3. **Has the system tried to self-heal?** There is no self-heal available; the
   auto-override _is_ the self-heal, and this alert says it is disabled.
4. **Deduplicated against root cause?** One page per site per transition, not one
   per broken reference.
5. **Useful click target?** Tier-1: `/admin/bonus-chain-health`.

**Priority `high`, not `urgent`.** A broken chain is real and payroll-impacting,
but it is _slow-moving_ — it only becomes load-bearing at 08:30 PT on a payroll
morning. Waking Bill at 3am for a condition that has been true for a week and
will not bite until Tuesday is exactly what ADR-0037 exists to stop. Amber pages
at `default`. The 08:30 t3 page stays `urgent`, unchanged: at that point it _is_
an emergency.

**Fires on transition, not per poll.** `decideChainPage` follows the `decidePage`
pattern from `@/lib/mymrc/sync.ts`: page on the leading edge into an unhealthy
state, on any worsening (amber→red), and on a persisting failure once past 24h.
Never for green, and never for an improvement — recovery is not an alarm.

Prior state is read from a Postgres ledger (`bonus_chain_health_runs`), **not**
from `publishNtfy`'s cooldown map. That map is per-process and every deploy
recreates the container, so an in-memory cooldown would re-page on every deploy.
The ledger also makes "the check stopped running" visible, the same reason
`doc_ingest_sweep_runs` always writes a row.

**A dropped page is recorded as dropped.** `runChainHealthSweep` inspects
`publishNtfy`'s returned outcome and only sets `paged = true` on `sent` /
`fallback-sent`. The 2026-08-05 lesson is that a page can vanish; a ledger that
claims someone was told when nobody was would launder that failure.

### 8. Reuse `dr3-vision-system`, do not mint a topic

Per ADR-0036 the topic convention is `<service-code>-<purpose>`, which would
suggest something like `dr3-vision-bonus`. Rejected: every application alert in
this repo publishes to `dr3-vision-system`, and as `stale-claim-scan.ts` puts it,
"a new topic nobody is subscribed to is a silent black hole." A new topic would
also need registering in `noc-master/data/ntfy-fallback-topics.yml` _and_ in
`FALLBACK_TOPIC_BY_PRIMARY`, or a primary failure returns `dropped`.
Differentiation is by fingerprint and tags.

## Alternatives considered

- **Make the t3 guard louder.** Rejected — it already fires `urgent`, and on
  2026-08-05 an urgent page was dropped outright. The problem is not volume; it
  is that the only channel is lossy and speaks too late.
- **Fix the seed and call it done (ADR-0019.3).** Necessary, insufficient. The
  seed alias was _this_ recurrence's cause. The class is "chain references drift
  and nothing looks", and a manual `/admin/users` deactivation would reopen it.
- **Block deactivating a user who holds a chain slot.** Attractive, and probably
  right eventually, but it only guards one entry path — it does nothing about
  seeds, migrations, or direct SQL, all of which caused real incidents here.
- **Auto-repair: silently repoint a dead actor at any active admin.** Firmly
  rejected. Signing payroll as an identity nobody chose is worse than missing a
  deadline, which is the reasoning behind the original t3 guard and it still
  holds.
- **Poll every 30s from the pill instead of a cron.** The pill _does_ evaluate
  live on each poll. But the pill only renders on `/` for a logged-in
  manager/admin, so it cannot alert at 06:30 on a morning when nobody has opened
  the app. The cron is the part that does not depend on someone looking.
- **A mutable single-row state table instead of a ledger.** Rejected — the
  history is what makes a stopped checker visible.

## Consequences

- The ADR-0019.1 §4 promise is now real. Amendment 2 of that ADR, which recorded
  it as unbuilt, is updated to point here.
- A dead chain reference is visible on a dashboard from the moment it is
  introduced, rather than at 08:30 PT on the morning it matters.
- `evaluateChainHealth` is pure, so the interesting logic is tested without a
  database (21 unit tests), including a regression test against the exact
  pre-2026-08-11 production shape — a seed regression that reinstates the dead
  alias fails the suite.
- The new linkage test asserts the indicator and the auto-override agree on the
  same fixture. Two independent readings of one chain that could disagree would
  be worse than one reading, because green-while-failing is the exact illusion
  being removed.
- One more cron container. It POSTs once a day and does two reads; the cost is
  negligible against a missed payroll.
- **Not addressed here:** the `[escalation] stranded ntfy dropped` failure itself.
  Why that publish failed on both primary and fallback while the ntfy server was
  demonstrably healthy is unexplained and worth its own investigation. This ADR
  reduces the _consequence_ of a dropped page — the state is still readable — but
  does not fix the drop.
- **Also not addressed:** `tierAutoOverride` increments `result.ntfyPublished`
  without checking `publishNtfy`'s outcome, so a dropped "actor unavailable" page
  is counted as published. The new sweep does check its own outcome; the
  escalation path was left alone to keep this change reviewable.
