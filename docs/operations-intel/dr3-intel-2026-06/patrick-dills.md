## 1. From the Eugene floor — what equipment and processes do you work with? Walk me through a typical shift.

I come in every morning at about 7am, I don’t clock in tho, I get on the cpu and check emails for any garbage pick up requests for the rail truck drivers that were requested after 4:30pm the previous day, if so I enter those into the Trailer Movement log,  then I check the mymrc portal for any requests for mattress delivery’s, if so I fill out the BOL’s for the delivery’s. After that I unlock the warehouse and do my walk through. I turn on all the lights. Open our main bay door to air out and cool it down inside for the processors before they get there, I then walk down to the far side of the warehouse house Turning on all the lights and turning on the styrofoam machine (it takes 30 mins to warm up to temp). I then go to the processors tables and I count in all of their mattresses that are in their lane, that way they can just come right in and get started.  About this time it’s 7:30 and it’s my alone time where I just sit in silence before clocking in and starting the day. Once I clock in I check in with Rick and we go over quickly what’s going on that day. I then go out on the floor and I run the forklift, pick up processors garbage, foam etc. unloading mattress trailers. Greeting and unloading public mattress drop off. Bringing more mattresses to the processors and writing how many mattresses are in each stack I bring them down on their processing paper.  I bring the processors more mattresses when they start to run low that way they don’t run out and then are standing and waiting. That isn’t being productive. Unloading Svdp fleet truck mattress drop offs. I also help wherever I’m needed. Metal baling usually. When trailers or retail mattress trucks come in with mattresses, after unloading, I fill out the BOL and take it into the office and enter it into our spreadsheets and the mymrc. This same thing continues all day. Along with filling up the wood bin outside with wood from wooden mattress platforms. If Rick is not there at the warehouse for any reason my day kind of looks the same but with more time in the office on the cpu and answering calls. Also throughout the day I’m walking the floor and observing all operations making sure everything is running smoothly and everyone is doing their job correctly just by observing that way they don’t feel like I’m micromanaging. If there is an area that isn’t running smoothly I observe whats going on and figure out where the problem is coming from and I fix it. Also throughout the day my company phone is ringing with customers wanting to pay for a mattress pick up. When that happens , I take the call and go into the office, open the register, get all the necessary information from the customer. Once that is complete I call dispatch where we confirm together the name of the customer, how much they paid, and what date the mattress pick up is for. At the end of the day at about 3:50pm, I do our daily end of shift inventory. At this time any more mattresses that come in are marked for the following day that way it doesn’t mess up inventory after I have done my count. I walk the floor and count each stack of mattresses. This is made very easy for us because as we start a new row of mattresses we spray paint the number of mattresses in that stack on the front of the stack. So as I add more stacks to it I spray paint the new number on the the stack added. Ex: first stack is 10, spray paint 10. Second stack is 12, spray paint 22. That way when I’m doing my inventory all I have to add up is the final number spray painted on the last stack. Once I get my final number I go around to each processing table. I ask them how many more they are going to process. I then count how many mattresses they have left over in their stack and then I add that left over number to the end of day inventory. I then look at their processing sheet and I add up every every stack of mattresses they received that day and then I subtract their leftover amount from that, giving me their processing total. I do that for all 3 processors. I then go and write down how many public mattress drop off’s we received that day. As we tally them one by one as they come in. I then take the processors papers and the ending inventory number and public drop number into the office. This is when Rick usually stands up and has me enter in all the end of day numbers into our spreadsheets and into the Mymrc and also entering into the new dr3 vision the processors bonus numbers. I also fill out a spreadsheet at the end of the day with the amount of money we collected for customers paying for mattress pick up’s and I email that to store deposits.  I tried to be as specific as possible like you asked but I’m sure I forgot a few things. But this is my normal day.  I love my job. I am blessed.

## 2. The bonus system — how does it look from the processor's side? What's working, what's not, what would you change?

From the processors side I believe they all love it. I never hear complaints. I think it’s all working.  I just think that there is one person who has been there about 4 years now almost 5 as a processor and he just doesn’t really care what he gets. Processing wise. We’ve tried talking to him constructively, and trying to get him excited to get his numbers up but just the motivation isn’t there. He knows that the bonus number is 50 so he only aims for about 15-30 over that. Where our others are in the hundreds and 90’s. We have seen this person get big numbers before to make up for days that he’s called in. Not sure how to fix motivation.

## 8. What about the daily routine frustrates you most? What would you fix first if you could?

Just the fact that running as a Skeleton crew is ok. It puts a lot of work on a couple people and when this starts to happen is when injuries start to happen. And people get burnt out. It’s like if one person calls in sick, it throws the whole day sideways and we have to make personnel moves around the warehouse and we say “ok what’s the least important machine to run today? Ok let’s move that person to fill the spot of the person that called in.”  Or if the one person we have running the 2 metal machines, and taking over for me when I go on breaks(Chris) call in sick, or leaves early or is about to go on maternity leave, then that means I now have to run the floor, deal with making sure processors are taken care of, unload trailers, take phone calls, receive public drop mattresses and fleet truck and retailer mattresses and run both metal machines. It’s a lot of work. And when the processors are flying through mattresses it can get backed up very quickly. Right now we have 1 person running both garbage and foam balers. And one person running the metal sheer machine and the metal baler, 3 processors and Me running the floor. What we need is 2 people for the balers. One on each. And 2 people on metal. One on each. There is more than enough product for it. We just need 2 more people. That way if someone does happen to call in we don’t have to re think the whole operation. We recently just had a guy get fired. He was a machine operator. So I would think we would be needing to hire someone to replace him.   But I love my job. I’m blessed.
