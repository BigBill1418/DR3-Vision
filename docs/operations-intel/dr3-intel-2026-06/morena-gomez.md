## 1. As CA operations manager — what's your visibility across the CA facility today? What do you check daily, weekly, monthly? In what tools or formats?

As CA operations manager, I have visibility of a lot of different things during the day. I don't only look at one report. I check production, MRC and non-MRC dispatch the outlook calendar, inbounds, loads, holds, inventory, staffing, payroll/bonus information and any issue can affect the facility. 
Daily, I check what loads are coming in, what is scheduled in the Outlook calendar, what holds need to be scheduled, what was processed, what was saved for rebuilders, and if the numbers make sense with what actually happened on the floor. I also review some of Janette’s entries when needed, especially if something looks off or if we need to confirm the numbers.
I also enter the MRC dispatch information and review the holds so they can be scheduled into the Outlook calendar. A lot of my work is making sure the information in the system matches what is actually happening in the yard and in production.
Weekly, I look at production totals, bonus numbers, payroll items, dispatch issues, driver coverage, vendor pickups, and any open follow-ups. Monthly, I look more at the bigger picture, like production trends, inventory, capacity, staffing, and if we are meeting what MRC needs from us.
The tools I use are email, Excel, Outlook calendar, DR3 Vision/MRC information, payroll/bonus files, phone/text communication, and updates from Janette, the leads, drivers, vendors, and MRC contacts.

## 2. Where has the spreadsheet been wrong in ways you've caught? Tell me a few specific examples — what was wrong, how was it caught, what was the impact?

The spreadsheet has helped, but I have caught situations where the numbers did not match what I knew from the operation.
example is when processed units, saved units, or bonus numbers do not match what was actually handled on the floor. I usually catch that by comparing the spreadsheet/system numbers with what Janette reports, what I know from production, and what is being submitted for payroll or bonus.
Also, sometimes dispatch or holds need to be checked against the Outlook calendar because the spreadsheet by itself does not always show the full story. A load can be scheduled, late, missed, moved, or on hold, and if that is not updated correctly, the numbers can look wrong.
The impact is that wrong numbers can affect production reports, bonus, payroll, MRC reconciliation, inventory, and leadership’s understanding of what is really happening in California. It also takes extra time to go back and explain or correct it.

## 3. What cross-facility coordination work do you do that paper or email handles today? Material moves, shared resources, staffing, anything.

A lot of the cross-facility coordination is still handled by email, text, phone calls, and manual notes.

I coordinate between Stockton and Woodland for staffing, transportation, drivers, material moves, equipment, and operational support. With Stockton winding down, there has been a lot of communication about what needs to move, what is still pending, what material needs to be removed, and what Woodland needs to be ready for.

For MRC dispatch, I enter dispatch information, review holds, and schedule them into the Outlook calendar. I also follow up when trailers are late, missed, or when something changes. Sometimes the information is in the Outlook calendar, sometimes in email, and sometimes from a direct call or text, so I have to put the pieces together.

I also coordinate with HR, payroll, vendors, maintenance, drivers, and the facility team. This includes staffing coverage, payroll corrections, bonus information, inspection follow-ups, vendor pickups, fire drill items, equipment issues, and other facility needs.

The hard part is that information is spread in too many places. If someone is out or if something is missed in an email, it can affect the operation.

## 4. What dashboard would you want to see when you open Vision in the morning? What 5 things tell you "is CA OK today?"

What MRC loads are scheduled today, including times, holds, and anything late or not confirmed.

Current inventory and yard capacity, so I know if we have enough material or if we are getting too full.

Production status, including yesterday’s processed count, saved/rebuilder units, and today’s goal.

Staffing coverage, including if we have enough processors, leads, drivers, forklift coverage, and office support.

Alerts or exceptions, like missing entries, numbers that do not match, dispatch not matching the Outlook calendar, equipment down, late trailers, or something that needs follow-up.

For me, the dashboard should not only show if the number is good or bad. It should also show why. For example, if production is low because we did not have enough inbound material, that should be clear. Otherwise, it can look like the team did not perform, when the real issue was dispatch or material flow.

## 5. You've seen Vision's daily reconciliation reports recently. What did you do with that data? Was it useful? What would you change about it?

I used the daily reconciliation reports to compare what Vision was showing with what I already knew from production, dispatch, the Outlook calendar, and Janette’s entries.

It was useful because it helped show where the numbers did not match. It also helped us catch things earlier instead of waiting until payroll, bonus, or monthly reporting.
What I would change is that I would like the report to be more clear about what needs action. For example, it would help to show where the number came from, who entered it, when it was entered, and what changed. It would also help to have a place for notes, so if there is a reason for the discrepancy, we can document it right there.

I would also like the report to separate data entry issues from real operational issues. Sometimes the issue is not production. Sometimes the issue is that a load was late, a hold was not scheduled yet, the Outlook calendar was not matching, or the count was entered under the wrong date.

## 6. What systems and tools do you use most often in your work, and what do you wish you could replace?

The tools I use the most are email, Excel, Outlook calendar, DR3 Vision/MRC tools, payroll/bonus information, phone calls, text messages, and direct updates from Janette, leads, drivers, vendors, and MRC contacts.

I use the Outlook calendar for MRC dispatch scheduling and holds. I use Excel and other manual tracking for production counts, bonus review, payroll support, and reconciliation. I use email for approvals, vendor communication, HR/payroll items, inspections, maintenance, customer follow-ups, and facility issues.

Right now, Vision is not fully live yet. The bonus section is the part that is already live, and I am grateful for that because it is helping reduce some of the manual Excel work. I do not like having too many Excel sheets, especially when the same information has to be checked or entered in more than one place.

What I wish we could replace is entering or checking the same information in different places. Sometimes one item is in email, Outlook calendar, Excel, Vision, and also discussed by phone or text. That takes time and creates more chance for mistakes.

I do not think email can go away completely, but Vision could help reduce the back-and-forth if we had one place for production, dispatch, holds, saved units, bonus information, comments, discrepancies, and follow-up items.

## 7. Now that Stockton has wound down, what's the CA facility footprint going forward? Anything Vision needs to know about future expansion or contraction at Woodland?

Going forward, Woodland is becoming the main California operating facility. Stockton has wound down and is no longer receiving non-MRC material the way it was before. There are still close-out items for Stockton, like moving material, equipment, maintenance items, and making sure everything is finished correctly.

Vision needs to know that when Stockton winds down, the work does not just disappear. Some of that work, volume, coordination, and pressure moves to Woodland.

Woodland will need to handle more activity, including production, staffing coordination, dispatch support, equipment use, and possibly other material depending on the final plan. Woodland also has limits with yard space, trailers, staffing, equipment, and inbound flow.

If Vision is going to help us, it needs to be flexible with the facility changes. It should show what is active, what material belongs where, what has moved, what is pending, and what is no longer part of the operation.

## 8. What are we missing? What should we be looking at that we haven't asked about?

> Anything that didn't fit the questions above. Operational pain points, data quality issues, blind spots, ideas, concerns, side topics — whatever you think we should know.

I think one thing we are missing is the reason behind the numbers. The system should not only show that production was low or that something does not match. It should help explain why.

For example, low production can happen because there was no inbound material, equipment was down, staffing was short, the yard was full, trailers were late, or the number was entered wrong. Those are very different problems.

I also think Vision should show who entered or changed information, when it was changed, and why. That would help a lot when we are trying to audit numbers or fix discrepancies.

Another important thing is connecting dispatch, the Outlook calendar, production, holds, bonus, and reconciliation. Those pieces are all connected in real life, but right now we still have to check them manually in different places.

I would also like a place for operational notes and follow-ups. A lot of important information is in emails, texts, or verbal conversations. If someone is out, it is hard for another person to understand the full picture.

For me, Vision should be built for the people who enter and use the data every day, not only for leadership reports. It needs to be simple enough for the team to use correctly but detailed enough so leadership gets accurate information. The goal should be less manual correction, less repeated work, fewer Excel sheets, and better visibility for everyone.
